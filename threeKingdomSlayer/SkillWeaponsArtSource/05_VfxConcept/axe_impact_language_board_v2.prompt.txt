Create one impact-language comparison board for a high-definition pixel-art Three Kingdoms battlefield action game: a single 1536x1024 sheet holding four panels in a 2x2 grid. Each panel is one independent peak-impact instant of a heavy ground strike, seen close up, and the four panels exist to compare four different ways the earth can break. This sheet compares destruction language only; it is not a time sequence and not a production texture.

WHAT IS IDENTICAL IN ALL FOUR PANELS: the same warm yellow-brown battlefield soil seen from the same fixed oblique top-down 2.5D angle, the same camera height and framing, the same lighting, the same ground texture and colour, the same pixel rendering language. The impact area fills roughly 55 to 65 percent of each panel, centred, and the background is simplified plain ground with no scenery props and no clutter, so the damage stays the subject.

WHAT EACH PANEL SHOWS:
PANEL A - CRUSHED DEPRESSION. The centre, about a fifth of the impact area, collapses into an irregular crushed pit of broken soil and stone with a lumpy broken rim; around it four to six clods of earth and rock are crushed flat or lifted slightly at their edges; from the rim of the pit a few thick cracks burst outward.
PANEL B - SINGLE-BLADE SPLIT. The centre is cut by one short deep split with the clean weight of a heavy single-edged axe blow; the crack pattern is clearly asymmetric and stretches along the direction of the chop, with the branches on one side long and the branches on the other side short, so the whole shape leans.
PANEL C - BURST. A high-contrast broken core bursts open at the centre, six to eight thick cracks shoot out to the outer ring, their mid sections broken and offset like snapped stone, and a few small clods of earth are thrown outward from the core.
PANEL D - OFFSET SLABS. The ground at the centre is smashed into three to five thick slabs of earth and rock that have shifted and tilted against each other, each slab showing a clear fault edge where it broke; the cracks between them are few but very thick.

THE DAMAGE LANGUAGE IN ALL FOUR PANELS: the centre reads as a solid crushed and broken core of soil and stone, not a crossing of thin lines. About five to seven main cracks with a clear hierarchy, some thick and long, others short and secondary, and they never radiate evenly like a star. The main split has a slight direction and a slight asymmetry, as a single-edged axe blow would leave. A few small dirt clods, gravel and a bright lit edge on the broken fault surfaces show real material under force. Warm yellow-brown battlefield soil, deep cold gray-brown crack interior, a few gray-white lit soil and stone edges, high-definition pixel rendering with big hard-edged colour clusters and stepped silhouettes. The impression must be the physical weight of a heavy cold blue-gray steel single-edged giant axe hitting the ground. No weapon is drawn anywhere on this sheet.
