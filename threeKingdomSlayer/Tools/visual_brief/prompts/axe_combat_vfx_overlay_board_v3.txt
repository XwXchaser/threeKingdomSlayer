Create one combat VFX overlay concept sheet for an existing high-definition pixel-art Three Kingdoms battle screen: a single 1536x1024 board with six panels, three columns by two rows, read left to right and top to bottom.

This is a 2D/2.5D ground-overlay VFX design for the existing in-game combat screen, not an environment concept, not terrain destruction, and not a 3D geological fracture. Every effect must read as a flat sprite layered on the warm ochre battlefield ground, using the project's existing high-definition pixel combat language: a short gold-white impact core, restrained vermilion accents, thick cool gray-brown cracks, hard-edged colour clusters, and stepped silhouettes.

WHAT IS IDENTICAL IN ALL SIX PANELS: the same warm yellow-brown battlefield soil seen from the same fixed close oblique top-down 2.5D angle, the same camera, the same ground texture and the same lighting; the same effect position at the centre of the frame and the same effect size in every panel. The effect fills roughly 35 to 50 percent of the frame. The background ground is simplified, but it keeps the project's real warm soil colour, its pixel texture and its brightness relationship. The ground keeps its own lighting and its own texture in every panel; only the flat effect layer differs from panel to panel.

WHAT EACH PANEL SHOWS:
1. CRACKS ONLY. Five to seven thick cool gray-brown ground cracks with a clear hierarchy, some thick and long, others short and secondary, plus a few short branches. The whole crack set leans slightly in one direction, as a single-edged axe blow would leave it, and it never radiates evenly like a star.
2. CRACKS + GOLD-WHITE IMPACT CORE. The same cracks, plus a short gold-white impact core at the centre: a small concentrated hard-edged flash shape, like the hit spark of this game, not a round glowing ball and not a large white blob.
3. CRACKS + GOLD-WHITE CORE + VERMILION ACCENT. The same cracks and the same gold-white core, plus a few short vermilion accent strokes right around the core, the colour of this game's slash and impact effects. The vermilion stays a small accent close to the centre and never spreads across the ground.
4. CRACKS + A FEW BROKEN SOIL PIXELS. The same cracks, plus a small number of flat dark-brown soil pixels and tiny clods scattered close to the crack edges, drawn as flat pixel clusters with no volume, no floating slabs and no 3D rocks.
5. CRACKS + AXE-DIRECTION DRAG MARK. The same cracks, plus one short, thick, hard-edged ground scrape that reads as the direction of the single-edged axe blow: a compact straight drag, not a long light streak, not a sweeping arc and not a glowing trail.
6. COMBINED VERSION. The cracks of panel 1, the gold-white impact core of panel 2 with the vermilion accent of panel 3, the few broken soil pixels of panel 4 and the light directional drag mark of panel 5, all combined into one flat effect. This is the proposed final look.

HARD CONSTRAINTS FOR ALL SIX PANELS: everything is a flat layer lying on the ground plane. No vertical surfaces, no pit walls, no canyon, no crater, no raised blocks, no thick earth chunks, no terrain slices, no tilted rock plates, no 3D rock structures. The centre is a flat high-contrast impact area, never a hollow and never a three-dimensional hole. The cracks are five to seven thick hard main cracks plus a few short branches, with clear hierarchy in thickness and length, leaning slightly in one direction and never evenly radial. Gold-white is used only for the short impact core and a few lit edges, vermilion only for a few short speed and impact accents; there is no full white slab, no neon and no magic glow. Everything is drawn in the project's high-definition pixel language: big hard-edged colour clusters, stepped silhouettes, a clear focal point, no realistic materials, no soft gradients, no airbrushed halo, no volumetric light and no uniform noise. No character, no axe, no enemy, no interface, no text, no label, no distant scenery and no scenery props in the background. This sheet compares flat combat VFX overlays for review only; it is not a final transparent texture and not an animation frame sequence.
